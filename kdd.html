01 · Overview

About the Project

The Community

KDD was founded in 2017 by a Korean developer in Vancouver with one wish: a space where Koreans from different fields could meet, share, and grow together. Today KDD runs regular meetups, mentoring sessions, and an annual conference co-hosted with the Korean Consulate.

My Role

As brand designer, I led the visual identity redesign end to end: defining the brand concept, designing the logo mark, establishing the color and type system, and producing templates for social media, events, and physical merchandise.

02 · Challenge

The Problem

"KDD had no unified visual identity. Every event, post, and printed piece looked different. This was especially critical ahead of their annual flagship event with the Korean Consulate."

01
Inconsistent Logo Usage
No defined rules for how the logo appeared across web, social, and print. This resulted in stretched, recolored, and misused versions everywhere.
02
No Color System
Event flyers used different palettes each time. There was no primary color, no secondary palette, and no rules for combination.
03
Typography Inconsistency
Korean and English type were treated separately with no hierarchy rules, causing visual noise across every touchpoint.

04 · Logo

Built on a Pixel Grid

The KDD wordmark is constructed on a precise pixel grid. The K carries a checkerboard pixel accent referencing digital craft and the "first pixel" concept. Both D's use geometric arcs of increasing width, suggesting growth and momentum from left to right.

05 · Color

The Palette

The primary palette is built around a vivid cobalt, a color that reads as both digital and authoritative, nodding to the tech sector without feeling corporate. It pairs with near-black and warm off-white for maximum contrast across all media.

07 · Outcome

What Shipped

What We Achieved

The rebrand launched at the 6th Annual KDD Tech Conference 2026, co-hosted with the Korean Consulate. KDD now has a consistent visual identity applied across social media and the conference, along with ready-to-use templates so anyone on the team can create social media posts, slides, or PowerPoint decks without starting from scratch.

What I Learned

Every asset needed to work beyond the initial launch. Making the system reusable and handoff-ready was just as important as making it look good.
